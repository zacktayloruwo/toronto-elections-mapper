# About this map

This site pairs two maps of Toronto's 585 census tracts: the **social geography** of
the city on the left, and election-day **mayoral election results** on the right. 
Hovering over a tract highlights it on both maps. The **correlation panel** reports 
how closely a candidate's vote share tracks each census variable, and how similar 
the selected candidate's electoral map is to other candidates' maps.

Created by **Prof. Zack Taylor**, Dept. of Political Science, University of 
Western Ontario. For more information, contact him at zack.taylor@uwo.ca.

## How to read it

Percentages are shares of the relevant census table total, shown in the hover
readout alongside the counts they are calculated from. Income, density, location,
and access measures are standardized within each census year, so a value of +1.0
means one standard deviation above the average neighbourhood that year.

Correlations are measured across tracts, not across people. A strong correlation
between a candidate's vote share and a neighbourhood characteristic does not mean
that individuals with that characteristic voted that way.

## Data sources

- **Census variables** are drawn from Statistics Canada censuses, 1996 to 2021,
  apportioned to 2021 census tract boundaries using the Canadian Longitudinal
  Tract Database crosswalks.
- **Election results** are poll-level mayoral results for 1997 through 2023,
  apportioned to 2021 census tracts. Only election-day votes are included, because
  advance votes cannot be assigned to a neighbourhood. (Election-day voting 
  subdivisions were decomposed into census dissemination blocks by their population
  share and then summed to 2021 census tract boundaries.)
- **Boundaries** are 2021 census tracts from Statistics Canada. Ward, old city
  and neighbourhood names are joined from the City of Toronto.
